The later-minus-earlier difference is 3.56 years when each citation link has equal weight, and 2.35 years when each opinion with at least one link has equal weight. The latter is the average of the individual opinion means, not the age of a typical citation.

Sensitivity to individual opinions

Removing one opinion at a time produces equal-opinion mean differences from 1.46 to 2.93 years, across 125 valid removals. Each removal begins with the full datasets. This range measures sensitivity to a single included opinion; it is not a confidence interval and does not address missing citations, omitted opinions, or several influential opinions acting together.

Definitions and limitations

Each distinct source-to-precedent pair contributes one citation age: source decision year minus cited decision year. Opinion means are calculated from those distinct links. Zero-link opinions remain in citation-count statistics; their age statistics are undefined and excluded from age summaries. A median of opinion means is different from a median across all links.

Describes the curated datasets only. Source selection, issue composition, extraction differences, and missing citations are not controlled. In particular, the 2004 data use Harvard archive opinion-block links and the 2024 data use official PDF extraction. The sensitivity check cannot establish a historical trend or explain why a difference occurs.
